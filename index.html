5. Questions you might ask

I tried to answer them before you asked.

Isn’t a bike dangerous?

Any vehicle carries risk. That is why I commit to a good ISI/DOT helmet, riding gloves and jacket, no night rides at first, and no speeding. Choosing a bike with dual-channel ABS also helps a lot.

Why not a cheaper scooter?

A scooter is a fine choice for city errands. But this bike is something I will keep for years, and I am ready to take care of it properly.

Is it too powerful for a beginner?

That is why the Hunter 350, Xpulse 200 and Ronin are on top of my list. They are friendly, easy to handle and forgiving while I learn. The bigger bikes stay as long-term dream options.

Who pays for fuel and servicing?

I will contribute from my allowance or earnings and keep a simple expense log that you can check any time.

What if I break the rules?

Then you can take the keys away, no argument. The promises above are a real agreement.
